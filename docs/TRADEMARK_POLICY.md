# Micelia Trademark Policy

**Version**: 1.0
**Effective**: 2026-05-24
**Mark**: MICELIA (and the Micelia mycelium logo, when registered)
**Owner**: Asociación Micelia para la Soberanía Computacional Cooperativa (in formation)
**Filings**: EUIPO classes 9 (software), 41 (education), 42 (IT services) — in process. USPTO — planned.
**Companion to**: `LICENSE`, `LICENSE.fsl`, `docs/LICENSING_STRATEGY.md`

---

## 1. Why a trademark policy

Micelia source code is licensed under AGPLv3 — anyone can fork it, modify it, redistribute it. That is the open-source contract and we honor it.

But the **name** "Micelia" is not part of the code. It is a separate asset: a trademark. The trademark exists so that when users see "Micelia" they get the actual Micelia — the cooperative project with its governance, its AI sovereignty policy, its mycelial federation. Not a fork that quietly removed those guarantees while keeping the name.

This policy explains what you can and cannot do with the name "Micelia" and the Micelia logo.

---

## 2. What is permitted (no permission needed)

You may use the Micelia trademarks freely in the following descriptive contexts:

### 2.1 Compatibility claims

- "Compatible with Micelia"
- "Works with Micelia v0.1"
- "Built on Micelia federation protocol"
- "Imports the Micelia Python SDK"

### 2.2 Educational and editorial use

- Writing articles, blog posts, papers, books, or research about Micelia
- Teaching courses where Micelia is discussed or demonstrated
- Comparative analyses ("Micelia vs Mastodon", "Why we chose Micelia over X")
- Talks at conferences referencing Micelia

### 2.3 Linking and citation

- Hyperlinking to micelia.org or to the Micelia repository
- Citing Micelia in bibliographies or `package.json` / `pyproject.toml` dependency lists
- Using the Micelia name in error messages or log lines of compatible software

### 2.4 Personal node identity

- Naming your own Micelia node anything you like, including names that contain "Micelia" descriptively ("Jessica's Micelia Node", "micelia-instance-42")
- Public dashboards identifying your node as part of the Micelia network

---

## 3. What requires permission

The following uses require explicit written permission from the Steward:

### 3.1 Commercial product names containing "Micelia"

- "Micelia Pro", "Micelia Enterprise", "Micelia Cloud", "Managed Micelia"
- Any SaaS offering whose product name contains "Micelia"
- Hardware products branded "Micelia" (e.g. a "Micelia Node Appliance")

### 3.2 Domain names containing "Micelia"

- micelia.io, micelia.app, micelia.cloud, getmicelia.com, etc.
- Subdomain uses like "micelia.bigcorp.com" if the subdomain offers a service that consumes the Micelia mark commercially

### 3.3 Logo modifications

- Creating derivative logos that combine the Micelia mycelium mark with other brands
- Using the Micelia logo as the primary identifier of a forked project
- Embedding the Micelia logo in commercial promotional material

### 3.4 Distribution under the Micelia name

If you redistribute the Micelia software under the name "Micelia", you must distribute it **unmodified** OR with patches that have been merged into the upstream Micelia repository. This is the Firefox / Iceweasel pattern: the code is free to modify (AGPLv3), but the name "Micelia" is reserved for distributions consistent with upstream.

For modified distributions:

- **Allowed**: distribute under a clearly different name (e.g. "Hyphae", "Fungaria") with a one-line statement "Based on Micelia (https://micelia.org)"
- **Allowed**: distribute as "Micelia Community Edition" if you maintain compatibility with upstream and accept the Micelia governance
- **Requires permission**: distribute under any name containing "Micelia" if you have removed, disabled, or modified the AI Sovereignty Policy enforcement, the cooperative governance, or the federation protocol

---

## 4. What is forbidden

These uses violate the trademark policy regardless of intent:

- Registering a domain name confusingly similar to a Micelia property in bad faith (cybersquatting)
- Selling merchandise (T-shirts, mugs, stickers) bearing the Micelia logo without a license agreement (note: the Steward will offer permission liberally for small-quantity community merchandise — just ask)
- Implying official Micelia endorsement when none exists
- Using the Micelia name to market AI services that violate the AI Sovereignty Policy (e.g. "Micelia powered by GPT-4 only")
- Distributing forked versions of Micelia under the same name with substantive changes to the federation protocol, license enforcement, or governance

---

## 5. The fork test

To know whether your use needs permission, apply the fork test:

> If a user saw your product and assumed it was the upstream Micelia cooperative project, would that assumption be wrong on any of the following dimensions?
>
> 1. Is the AI Sovereignty Policy enforced?
> 2. Is the cooperative governance respected?
> 3. Is the federation protocol implemented compatibly?
> 4. Is the AGPLv3 license honored for derivative works?
> 5. Are user data sovereignty defaults preserved?
>
> If yes to any → you need a trademark license OR you need a different name.

This test is more permissive than typical commercial trademarks. Educational forks, research forks, and small experimental forks that deviate from upstream are encouraged — they just shouldn't be called "Micelia".

---

## 6. How to request permission

Send an email to trademark@micelia.org (mailbox to be provisioned with the EUIPO filing) including:

1. The exact name or logo use you propose
2. The purpose and target audience
3. How users will distinguish your offering from official Micelia
4. Your commitment to honor the items in §5 (fork test)

The Steward will respond within 30 days. Most reasonable requests are granted at no cost, often with light conditions (e.g. "include a Made with Micelia badge linking to micelia.org").

## 7. Trademark license offered for cooperatives

Other worker cooperatives, social-economy entities, and non-profits using Micelia in mission-aligned ways may use the Micelia name without restriction beyond standard attribution. The Steward will pre-approve such uses by issuing a public "Cooperative Trademark Allowance" upon notification. This is intended to make cross-cooperative collaboration frictionless.

---

## 8. Enforcement

The Steward will pursue enforcement only against uses that cause material confusion to users, particularly:

- Commercial extractive forks that reduce protections users expect from Micelia
- Cybersquatting on Micelia domain variants
- Fraudulent claims of official endorsement

Enforcement actions begin with a private notice and a 30-day cure period. The Steward does not aim to be a trademark bully — the goal is preserving the meaning of "Micelia" for the users who trust the name.

---

## 9. Modifications to this policy

This policy may be updated. Material changes (e.g. tightening restrictions) require a 90-day notice and a vote of Active Nodes per the procedure in `docs/CLA.md` §4. Loosening changes may be enacted by the Steward unilaterally with public notice.
